How it works

The model

Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM) trained on daily NOAA sea temperature since 1985, at 5 km, and 17,332 diver surveys from the Global Coral Bleaching Database. It predicts the chance that at least 30% of a reef's corals bleach.

Tested on years it never saw

Trained on surveys up to 2013 and scored on 2016 to 2020. It ranks bleaching better than NOAA's standard heat alert: 0.935 against 0.906 AUROC, on 2,771 surveys.

Checked against new surveys

MERMAID, WCS's open reef survey platform, holds 201 bleaching surveys from 2021 to 2025 in 10 countries, all after the training data ends. The model ranks them at 0.84 AUROC for 30% or more of corals bleached.

A bleaching year

A year in which the model put the reef past 50% risk in its hottest month. Fast-growing corals need 10 to 15 years to recover (Hughes et al. 2018), so a reef holds if this happens 2 or fewer years in 10.

Heat-tolerant coral

"Needs tougher coral" means the reef bleaches too often today, but would hold if restored with coral bred or selected to cope with water a little warmer (0.5 °C by default). You can change that target with the bars in the side panel.

Ranking places to restore

Among reefs that need tougher coral, places rank higher when local pressure (fishing, runoff, coastal development, tourism, industry) is low and when nearby reefs supply plenty of coral larvae.

Limits

This is a screening tool, not a site survey. Scores cover 5 km reef cells, not individual reefs, and say nothing about depth, water quality on the day, or local management.
